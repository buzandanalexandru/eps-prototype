# Proposal

Step 5: On-sale engine

**Demo act:** Act 2 (the live on-sale): the waiting room that 100,000 fans join before opening, the fair shuffle at opening, the admission dial, bots quietly sent to the back, and stock that never oversells.

**Features covered:** E1 sale settings screen, E2 waiting room with fair shuffle, E3 signed queue passes, E4 admission rate dial, E5 silent bot scoring, E6 caps per person, E12 risk-based OTP before payment (Real, simulated code).
**Decisions covered:** O1 queue (pre-queue, shuffle, rate), O2 sharded stock with timed holds and a reconciler. Builds on U2 (Redis keys prefixed by organiser and event), P1 (snapshots) and step 4's checkout, whose inventory interface this step re-implements.

## Why

A 100k-fan drop is decided before checkout: who gets in first, how fast, whether bots crowd out fans, and whether stock holds up without a single hot key (festival-tech section 10, patterns 1, 3, 4 and 5; Ticketmaster Smart Queue). EPS today has none of this; step 4's checkout is correct but would be overrun. This step makes the front door fair and the stock safe, so step 6 can point the simulator at it.

## What Changes

- A sale settings screen per sale moment: opening time, pre-queue opening, queue on or off, admission rate, the cap on fans shopping at once, hold length, caps per order and per person, bot defence and the OTP switch with its quantity threshold.
- A waiting room: fans who arrive before opening join a pre-queue; at opening they get random positions; later arrivals join behind in order. One place per account or session, kept across reloads and tabs.
- Admission through a live dial (fans per minute, pause allowed) and a limit on concurrent checkout sessions, each admitted fan receiving a signed pass bound to their session that checkout requires.
- Silent bot scoring from request behaviour; suspects are moved to the back after the shuffle, never shown an error, so bots learn nothing.
- Caps per person across every sale moment of an event, by account or normalised guest email, enforced when stock is held and again when payment succeeds.
- Risk-based OTP: when the organiser switches it on, only buyers whom scoring marks as risky or who buy above the threshold are asked for a one-time code (simulated and shown on screen) before payment.
- Sharded stock: each capacity pool split across several Redis counters, holds taken by atomic Lua scripts with their own expiry, and a reconciler that compares Redis with Postgres and repairs drift.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `sale-settings`: the per-sale settings, their validation and live changes.
- `waiting-room`: pre-queue, shuffle, positions, admission rate and active-session limit, signed passes and the checkout gate.
- `bot-defence`: silent scoring and where suspects go, caps per person, risk-based OTP.
- `inventory`: sharded holds, release, commit, sold-out detection and reconciliation.

### Modified Capabilities
None. The checkout's need for a pass is written here in `waiting-room`; the sharded engine keeps step 4's hold behaviour (`checkout`) unchanged.

## Impact

- **Go:** `cmd/gateway` gains the queue join, status and pass endpoints, the admitter loop and bot scoring; `cmd/core` gains pass verification, caps, OTP and the sharded inventory engine; `cmd/worker` gains the shuffle job, shard expiry sweeper and the reconciler.
- **Sales schema:** `sales`, `sale_settings` (versioned), `queue_entries` (audit of positions and admissions, written in batches), `cap_ledger`, `otp_challenges`, `inventory_adjustments`.
- **Redis:** queue, pass, shard, cap, rate-limit and score keys (design D-3).
- **API contract:** sale settings, queue, pass and OTP paths.
- **Next.js:** Studio sale settings screen with the live rate dial; the fan waiting room page (static shell, polls the gateway); the OTP step inside the locked checkout.
- **Helm:** gateway HPA on requests per second; Ed25519 pass signing key generated by CI into a Kubernetes secret.
- **No new dependencies.**

## Status

Done. Merged as `eaf790f` (PR #7, squash).

**What was built:**
- **Sale settings**, edited live from Studio through the contract (versioned saves; pause, rate and delete as their own actions; accounts-only).
- **The waiting room:**
  - a pre-queue;
  - a resumable shuffle at opening, with an audited seed;
  - first in, first out for later arrivals;
  - honest positions, with estimates from the admissions measured;
  - queue passes signed per session with Ed25519 by the gateway, whose private key stays on the gateway only.
- **Silent bot scoring:**
  - a shared address alone can trigger a one-time code at most, never moving a fan to the back;
  - IPv6 is grouped by /64;
  - fast polling is measured per tab.
- **One-time codes for risky sessions**, as a simulated flow.
- **Caps per person** across account, guest and verified email, the stricter of the sale and catalogue caps.
- **Sharded stock counters** behind step 4's Inventory interface:
  - commits share a lock and resizes take it alone;
  - stock rebuilds from Postgres and is reconciled against it;
  - step 4's Redis state is cleared on takeover.
- **Infrastructure:**
  - Redis at 1 GiB, with its append-only file on a block-storage volume;
  - the worker at 2 replicas under leases;
  - metrics and latency histograms for step 6;
  - k6 scripts for inventory and the queue.

**Deviations (design D-12 and D-13):**
- The shop calls the gateway through its own `/api/shop` proxy, and status polls skip the core token.
- A Vercel config turns off deployments for `wip/**` branches: the daily Hobby deployment limit was used up during this step.
- k6 targets were measured, not met:
  - 1,000 joins and 2,000 polls a second against the 5,000 and 10,000 targets;
  - about 900 holds a second against 1,000.
  
  Both are recorded as measured targets for steps 6 and 12.
- Documented only: the one-time code is a simulated flow, and a resume link carries a held order by design. The guest-identity residual risk is limited by per-address limits and the accounts-only setting.
